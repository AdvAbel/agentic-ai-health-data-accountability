Agentic AI — Health Data Accountability Self-Assessment

What this is: a self-assessment heuristic to help you think through accountability and privacy risk in an agentic AI system that processes health data. It reflects the analysis in the accompanying research project's /docs folder.

What this is not: this is not a certified compliance tool, legal advice, or a substitute for a proper legal or security audit. It does not guarantee compliance with any law. No claims are made about its adoption or accuracy in any specific deployment — use it as a prompt for questions worth asking, not as a verdict.
§1

Attribution clarity

Can you point to a specific human or organisation responsible for each action the agent takes?

§2

Lawful basis for each processing action

Not just "do we have consent" — but does the specific action the agent takes fall within what was consented to, or a specific legal exception?

§3

Sensitivity handling

Health data inferences (e.g. a mental health flag, a diagnosis) often carry more sensitivity than the raw data they're drawn from.

§4

Human oversight quality

A human "in the loop" is not the same as a human with meaningful control. (See: meaningful human control.)

§5

Breach and incident readiness

Can you actually detect when the agent has done something it shouldn't have?
