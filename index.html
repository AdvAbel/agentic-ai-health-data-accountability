Legal accountability for agentic AI systems processing sensitive health data

A research project developed alongside an LL.M. thesis in data privacy and information technology law. It asks what happens to a Data Fiduciary's accountability under India's data protection framework when an autonomous, agentic system — not a human — decides how health data gets processed. This is a work in progress and will grow as the research does.

Why this exists

Agentic AI systems don't just respond to instructions — they plan, retrieve data, choose actions, and act with limited human input at each step. Data protection law, in India and elsewhere, was written on the assumption that a human or organisation fixes the purpose and means of processing in advance.

This project traces where that assumption breaks down when the data in question is a patient's health information, and offers a practical way to check for the gap in a real deployment.
